The portfolio can be a unique experience for each visitor. I'm thinking if there's a way to use the session cookie, or the visitor's ip address, or something else, unique to them, that affects certain parameters on the visuals elements or components of the site. Maybe something with a hashing algorithm or encryption of some sort that alters the interface (visually but not necessarily functionally... ...unless it's possible to make a site that adapts to each user as well, sort of updating its parameters or even certain parts of the code(?) in real time based on the visitor's input and interaction?). This might be ridiculous or impractical. I haven't thought it all the way through yet. But basically something that would display my past work, while also in real-time, performing. Or showing a working example in an immediately tangible, experiential way. Especially through sound and visualization. So, an example for now might be as simple as running the visitor's ip address through a hash function that returns a hash value that gets converted or applied to parameter inputs on a basic web audio synth component, or color/transparency, theme, even font choices. It would have to happen on entering because it probably shouldn't change drastically say from one view to another. Maybe a landing page with a quick welcome message and a button to enter. That button click would be the event that triggers the SHA creation which sets the component parameters and when it renders, the visitor gets a beautiful experience that no visitor will get. And depending on how it works, even they may never get that again.

Then I was thinking it could also be very secure if we're already dealing with a hash function. Maybe those component parameters could also in some way, once they're set, could each be fed through a hash function/or through separate ones. and then each resulting hash gets pushed to an array which gets randomized and fed back - in the new, random order, into some sort of authentication/locking mechanism that locks that session at that point. From there, the visitor is sort of insulated and the only way to infiltrate/have the same exact exerience, would be to have the same ip address to get the same parameters, with the same salt, the same randomization outcome - but then the final output would still be different unless each person performed the same exact actions, same number of times, clicks, swipes, pages visited, key strokes, mouse pointer position. It could even go as far as eye tracking but not for this case. but to make a unique experience even more secure - as in non-reproducible, you could also include date/time/location data as part of the salting of the hashes. I'm not sure what this woud realistically do for the security of the site and the visitor while visiting. Or even for the experience aspect, but the experience part is I guess the one I'd be more focused on using it for right now. So creating this unique experience. The result of the final hash key (set of unique hashes fed into a mechanism in a random order) sets up the initial environment - which also doubles as a sort of canvas or backdrop, then certain data/metrics are tracked and applied as visual and audible media to that canvas/backdrop. So like, if you're the 183rd visitor to the site, that is going to affect something like color or texture or some other kind of property like that. So each key metric that one might find interesting (how much time spent on a page, how many mouse clicks total, number of skipped tracks, most played tracks, length listened, interactions, total time spent on the site before clicking the exit button, which could be something like a "Time to Say Goodbye" button, and when the visitor clicks that button, the screen goes dark and the begins to play back "the metrics/the data tracked during their vistit". Or if it takes some time to process and render the whole thing, a simple message can appear that sort of fades in and out slowly, a sentence at a time. It could say something about how we've reached the end of this journey/experience, thank you for taking the ride. Without you, this wouldn't exist... and then the the final 'showcase' begins. The sounds and visuals emerge from the darkness, gradually or suddenly, just based on the hash values and data/metrics involved. After about 5 seconds maybe, another set of short messages could begin - the same way as the first. It could say something like, as a parting gift, or as a symbol or token of appreciation I'm leaving you with something uniquely yours, or unique to this moment. - something like that. Then explain like, what you're seeing and hearing is the lifecycle of your visit. It's an artistic, or stylized expression of a moment. The results or consequences of every choice and step you made from the time you clicked enter/hello/begin, to the time you clicked exit/goodbye/end - things that you controlled, combined with things beyond your control. Every action and every inaction meant something, whether you were aware of it or not. Your relatively brief visit was a collaboration from the start. We were co-creators throughout that cycle and this is what we created together. It's the evidence, the proof that it happened. It's the historical artifact, the record of it's whole existence. It's a bunch of data points and metrics, mapped to various arbitrary parameters, driven by arbitrary or random logic in a process that returns all that data as something you can see and hear in a new way. It's the output of a thread on a single process where the only constant is change, chaos, turned to order, back to chaos, back to order - energy, motion, electricity, momentum, consumption, conversion, action, reaction, perception, contemplation, integration, stillness, rest - forward and backward. It's history repeating, it's the impermanent echo of your effort and attention, the ghost of your curiosity, it's your life flashing before your eyes. it's a memory. Remember?
